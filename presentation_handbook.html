Executive Research Summary

Urban water bodies in developing economies face chronic, unmonitored contamination from industrial discharges, sewage runoff, and heavy metals. This project introduces a fully autonomous, cost-effective environmental surveillance platform. It integrates 5 physical/chemical IoT sensors, computes standard Weighted Arithmetic Water Quality Index (WA-WQI) in sub-seconds, detects multivariate chemical dumping via unsupervised Isolation Forests, and provides 24-hour predictive trajectories using Random Forest Regressors deployed over a serverless GIS dashboard.

2. Scientific Methodology & Mathematical Modeling

Raw sensor values cannot directly convey overall water health to policymakers and citizens. Our platform implements the internationally recognized Weighted Arithmetic Water Quality Index (WA-WQI) formula, cross-referenced with WHO guidelines and the Department of Environment, Bangladesh (ECR) surface water limits.

Weighted Arithmetic WQI Formulation

$$\text{WQI} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} q_i \cdot w_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i}$$

Where $q_i$ represents the quality rating scale for the $i$-th parameter ($q_i = 100 \times \frac{|V_{actual} - V_{ideal}|}{V_{standard} - V_{ideal}}$) and $w_i$ is the unit analytical weight inversely proportional to the recommended allowable concentration.

Parameter Benchmarks & Relative Weights Table

Sensor Parameter Unit Ideal Value ($V_{ideal}$) Permissible Standard ($V_{std}$) Unit Weight ($w_i$) Environmental Significance
Dissolved Oxygen (DO) mg/L 14.0 $\ge 5.0$ 0.25 Primary bio-indicator for fish & aquatic respiratory survival.
Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) ppm 0.0 $\le 500.0$ 0.25 Measures dissolved inorganic salts, heavy metals, and industrial runoff.
pH Level pH 7.0 $6.5 - 8.5$ 0.22 Corrosiveness/toxicity; drops severely during chemical or acid discharge.
Turbidity NTU 0.0 $\le 5.0$ 0.18 Light penetration & suspended sediment from dredging and raw sludge.
Water Temperature °C 25.0 $10.0 - 35.0$ 0.10 Regulates metabolic rates and oxygen saturation capacity.

WQI Classification Benchmarks & Health Risk Mapping

Tier 1: WQI $\le 25$ Good / Potable

Meets all WHO drinking water criteria. Safe for drinking, aquatic breeding, and unrestricted domestic use.

Tier 2: WQI $26 - 50$ Moderate

Suitable for recreational use, irrigation, and resilient fish species. Requires conventional filtration for potable use.

Tier 3: WQI $51 - 75$ Poor / Polluted

Degraded ecological conditions. Requires extensive industrial effluent treatment. Direct human consumption prohibited.

Tier 4: WQI $> 75$ Critical / Hazardous

Severe biological dead-zone. Toxic chemical shock, zero fish survival, high risk of acute environmental toxicity.

4. Anticipated Faculty Questions & Winning Answers

Here are the 5 most probing questions university professors are guaranteed to ask, along with your scientifically grounded responses:

Q1. Why did you choose Isolation Forest instead of simple if-else threshold rules?
Winning Answer: "Honorable Professor, traditional thresholding only checks individual parameters independently. In a real industrial discharge, water might have a pH of 6.7—which is technically within the safe threshold of 6.5–8.5—but if Dissolved Oxygen drops simultaneously to 1.8 mg/L and Turbidity surges to 75 NTU, a threshold system fails to trigger. Isolation Forest operates in a 5-dimensional feature space. It recursively partitions random sub-samples; because anomalies require significantly fewer splits to isolate, it detects complex multivariate pollution shocks with zero labeled training data."
Q2. How do you handle sensor drift, calibration errors, and bio-fouling in real river water?
Winning Answer: "That is a crucial practical engineering issue. We address it through a hybrid hardware-software approach: On the hardware level, we implement 3-point buffer calibration (pH 4.01, 7.00, 10.01) and active temperature compensation using the DS18B20 digital sensor. On the software level, our ML pipeline implements 3-hour rolling averages and median filtering. This distinguishes slow, continuous calibration drift from sudden, sharp physical pollution discharges."
Q3. Why did you select Random Forest Regressor rather than Deep Learning models like LSTM or GRU?
Winning Answer: "For tabular environmental time-series of this scale, Random Forest provides three distinct advantages: First, LSTMs require tens of thousands of continuous epochs and easily overfit on seasonal drift. Second, by engineering 1-to-6 hour lag features and rolling standard deviations, Random Forest captures non-linear temporal dynamics without gradient vanishing issues. Third, inference takes under 5 milliseconds with negligible memory overhead, making it ideal for cloud serverless deployments."
Q4. How does the Weighted Arithmetic WQI model prevent one high parameter from masking others?
Winning Answer: "In the Weighted Arithmetic formulation ($WQI = \sum q_i w_i / \sum w_i$), the assigned weights are inversely proportional to the recommended limits. For critical biological survival indicators like Dissolved Oxygen and TDS, we assigned maximum weights of 0.25 each. When DO drops or TDS spikes, its individual sub-index $q_i$ scales aggressively, overriding secondary parameters and immediately driving the composite score into the 'Poor' or 'Critical' tier."
Q5. What happens if we scale this deployment to 100+ IoT nodes along the entire river network?
Winning Answer: "The platform was architected for horizontal scalability from Day 1. The FastAPI backend is completely asynchronous and non-blocking. Database transactions use indexed foreign keys and connection pooling compatible with managed PostgreSQL or Amazon RDS. On the frontend, Leaflet uses canvas-based GeoJSON clustering to render hundreds of spatial nodes at 60 frames per second."
